# Features

What `awacs.sh` does, grouped by area. Defaults in parentheses are the script's; `/etc/awacs.conf` overrides them ([configuration.md](configuration.md)). Log lines are quoted as printed; `N`, `NAME` and `ID` stand for the values the script fills in, and `wlan0` for the interface name.

## Connectivity and recovery

Internet check. Every `TICK` seconds (10): a ping to 8.8.8.8, a ping to 1.1.1.1, an HTTP request to `connectivitycheck.gstatic.com/generate_204` that must answer 204 and, when `SITE_URL` is set, a POST to the site endpoint that must answer 400. A captive portal answers 200 or 302 and counts as offline. The same four steps also run in a patient form where a busy link would otherwise be misread: three pings per target 0.3 s apart with a 5 s wait each, and 8 s limits on the HTTP steps. A failed quick check earns one patient check only when the interface sent 16384 bytes or more while it was failing, and only once per outage.

Recovery start. After `NET_FAIL_TICKS` (3) failed checks in a row the daemon logs `internet lost on HomeNet - router still answers, engaging` once (`router silent too`, or `router none (not associated)`: one gateway ping on that tick says which) and starts recovery, repeated every tick until `internet restored: NAME - down 5 min, 3 recovery runs` (the duration from the loss line and the recoveries it took; every step a recovery repeated follows as `repeated N more times during the outage (last at HH:MM): ...`). On that tick, while the gateway still answers and no patient check has failed in this outage, one patient check runs first: when it passes, nothing is torn down and the local file records `internet answers late, not never - slow link, no recovery (${NET_FAIL_TICKS} quick checks timed out)`. The site reads the episode: `uplink answering late on HomeNet - 3 quick checks timed out, the patient check passed, no recovery` at the first late pass, `internet answered late N more times in the last hour on HomeNet - slow link, no recovery` at most hourly while it lasts, `uplink answers normally again on HomeNet - answered late N times over 26 min` after about five minutes of quick answers, and `internet answered late N more times before this loss on HomeNet - slow link` when a loss ends it. Recovery starts 40 to 63 seconds after a loss on a link whose gateway has gone silent, and about 72 to 83 seconds after one on a link whose router keeps answering. A daemon that starts offline begins recovery at once, `no internet at start on HomeNet - router still answers, engaging`; one that starts online says `online at start via HomeNet`.

Gentle opening. Each recovery re-enables every stored network, re-arms hidden-network probing, asks the base layer to reassociate and waits up to `ASSOC_WAIT` seconds (25) for an association and an IPv4 address before anything is torn down. While the gateway answers, the first recovery of an outage skips the reassociate and keeps the live association: the network the device is on is left out of the candidates and is the one it returns to. The second recovery of the same outage reassociates anyway. A network the device already rides with an address is proven where it stands, never re-activated. The opening is said once per outage: `router answers on HomeNet - keeping the link, trying the other stored networks first`, `router answers on HomeNet but forwards nothing - reassociating anyway this time` (wpa, the second recovery) or `router not answering - waiting up to 25 s for wlan0 to reconnect`.

Measured choice. Every stored network on the air is activated in turn (`trying NAME`; a failure is `could not connect to NAME: REASON - trying the next`, the reason being `never associated`, `associated but got no address`, `refused - wrong password?`, `not found on the air`, `timed out after 25 s`, `NetworkManager not answering`, `NetworkManager: <its own message>` or `linked but no internet`) and probed with a `PROBE_KB` (200) kilobyte upload to `PROBE_URL` or, when that is empty, the site endpoint, until one uploads at four times the floor: `candidate [ID] NAME uploads at N kbps` (a probe the target refused is `upload probe failed on NAME - the probe target accepted nothing (site or endpoint down?), counting it as 0 kbps`). The best one is announced and kept: `switching to NAME (N kbps, the fastest candidate)`, then `switched to NAME (upload N kbps)`. A candidate at four times the floor ends the walk and the switching line adds ` - plainly fast, over 4x the F kbps floor, probing stopped at it`; a losing candidate taken because the way home failed says so, `switching to NAME (N kbps) - it delivered internet, HOME: REASON`. Signal strength alone never decides.

Recovery ladder. One rung per round, each aimed at a different layer. wpa: `recover L1: radio bounce` (interface down and up), `recover L2: restart dhcpcd (respawns the REAL per-interface supplicant)`, `recover L3: reload brcmfmac firmware (the 3-second cure a wedge needs)`. nm: `recover L1: radio bounce` (the interface's own rfkill switch), `recover L2: re-kick NetworkManager on wlan0` (`nmcli device reapply`, then disconnect and connect), `recover L3: restart NetworkManager + reload WiFi firmware`. Each rung line is said once per outage; a rung whose command failed adds `recover L1 did not complete: wlan0 would not come back up - radio not bounced`, `recover L2 did not complete: dhcpcd and wpa_supplicant both refused to restart - supplicant not respawned`, `recover L3 did not complete: brcmfmac would not unload - firmware not reloaded` or, on nm, `recover L3 did not complete: NetworkManager refused to restart - firmware reload follows`. An interface that vanishes while the daemon runs is said at the recovery start: `interface wlan0 has vanished - WiFi hardware or driver gone, rung L3 reloads the driver (a USB dongle needs replugging)`.

Emergency networks. `SAFETY_NET` entries (name and password, in `/etc/awacs.conf`) are tried after the stored networks and before any open network, whether or not the scan shows them: `trying emergency networks (N listed)`, then `connected to EMERGENCY network: NAME (upload N kbps) - stored networks stay armed, home again when one returns`. Each entry's fate is said once per outage: `emergency network NAME skipped: password must be 8-63 characters or 64 hex digits` (nm) or `emergency network NAME skipped: name or password carries a quote or backslash the supplicant cannot take` (wpa), `could not create the temporary entry for NAME - skipping it`, `emergency network NAME refused: REASON - trying the next`, and the closing `no emergency network delivered (N tried, M skipped)`.

Open networks. With `OPEN_NETWORKS="yes"` (the default) open networks come last, one attempt per name, skipping signals under -80 dBm (wpa) or 25 % (nm): `trying open networks as last resort (N open networks heard)`, then `connected to OPEN network: NAME (upload N kbps) - stored networks stay armed, home again when one returns`; a portal is `open network NAME linked but no internet (captive portal?) - trying the next`, and a walk without a win closes with `no open network delivered internet (N heard, M tried, K linked without internet)`.

Temporary entries. An emergency or open network is a temporary entry: on wpa a network id in the live supplicant, never saved; on nm a keyfile under `/run/NetworkManager/system-connections/` named `awacs-crutch-*` or `awacs-safety-*`, `autoconnect=false`, mode 0600. Its id is written to `/run/awacs/open_id` before the attempt, so the next daemon removes it if this one dies; it is removed as soon as a stored network is current again, `back on a stored network: HomeNet - temporary network NAME removed`; a recovery that finds the device still on one says `dropping temporary network NAME - it stopped delivering`, and a start that finds one `removed the temporary network NAME left by the previous run` (a WARN ending `- the device was still on it, recovery follows` when the device still rode it).

Hidden networks. On wpa the daemon sets `scan_ssid 1` on every stored network at start, at every recovery and after rungs L2 and L3, at run time only. On nm the profile must carry `802-11-wireless.hidden yes`; the daemon does not write it.

## Outage classification and the reboot condition

Gateway test. Before any rung, after every visible stored network has been activated and found without internet, the daemon pings the default gateway. An answer means the link works and the fault is upstream: `outage looks external: the router answers, the fault is upstream (round N) — waiting, not rebooting`. The other external verdicts name their sign too: `outage looks external: NAME linked but no internet, the fault is behind that network (round N) — waiting, not rebooting`, `outage looks external: none of your K stored networks is on the air, N others heard (round N) — waiting, not rebooting`, and `outage looks external (round N) — waiting, not rebooting` when NetworkManager was busy; each once per outage, later rounds counted. No rung runs, the reboot timer stays at zero, emergency and open networks are still tried, and the round waits 20 seconds; a way home that fails on that branch is said once per outage: `could not return to NAME: REASON - stored networks re-enabled, waiting 20 s`.

Three signs that the fault is on the device. With the gateway silent and no address this round, any one of these starts the reboot timer: a stored network is on the air and cannot be joined; the radio hears nothing; the stored list is empty. The verdict is said once per outage per sign, `fault looks on the device: HomeNet, OfficeNet on the air but this device cannot join - recovery follows` (or `the radio hears no network at all`, `the stored network list cannot be read`, `the stored network list reads empty`), and logged at DEBUG level as `fight round N: ME evidence (LINK_OK=0, gw unreachable, auth_failing=N)`; the last `N` is 1 when the credentials sign is present, otherwise 0.

Wrong password. wpa marks the network `TEMP-DISABLED`; on nm the activation fails with a credentials message. The daemon logs `association refused by HomeNet - wrong password? (recovery continues, reboot stays off)` (the refusing networks by name; `association refused - wrong password? (recovery continues, reboot stays off)` when none is known), resets the timer and keeps the ladder running. An `nmcli` that does not answer is said once per streak: `NetworkManager is not answering nmcli (exit 8) - stored networks cannot be tried until it is restarted (recover L3)`.

Reboot condition. A reboot needs `REBOOT_AFTER_MIN` minutes (30) of uninterrupted on-device evidence with no healthy tick and no credentials sign; on nm NetworkManager must also have settled in disconnected or failed on two consecutive rounds, or `nmcli` must be exiting 8 after rung L3. The first arming of the timer in an outage is `reboot clock armed - the device reboots after 03:41 unless the internet returns or the fault reads external`; on nm a later pick-up by NetworkManager clears it with `reboot clock cleared - NetworkManager picked the device up again`. The reboot itself is `rebooting now: wedged 30 min (HomeNet, OfficeNet on the air but this device cannot join) - reboot 1 for this fault, the story continues after the boot`, then `sync` and `reboot`; the timer then restarts from zero, and the reboots of one fault are counted in `/var/log/awacs.log.reboots` until a healthy tick. The knob rejects 0: the reboot can be delayed, not disabled.

Self-reboot memory. The reboot line and the whole outage story sit in the spool, which is tmpfs and would die with the reboot. Before rebooting the daemon copies the spool to `/var/log/awacs.log.spool` and writes `/var/log/awacs.log.reboot` (the time, the recovery runs, the boot id, the sign). The next start puts the copy in front of its own lines and says `starting after the reboot AWACS ordered at 2026-01-01 03:41 - wedged 30 min (HomeNet, OfficeNet on the air but this device cannot join), 7 recovery runs before it`; the first flush delivers the story, the reboot line, the start line and that WARN in order. A marker with the running boot's id means the `reboot` command failed: after 60 s alive, `the reboot AWACS ordered at 2026-01-01 03:41 did not happen - the reboot command failed, check the device`. A power cut leaves no marker.

## NetworkManager supervision

Backend detection. NetworkManager active or enabled makes the backend `nm`, after up to 60 seconds waiting for the service: `NetworkManager backend - AWACS supervises it (full capability)`. Otherwise the backend is `wpa`.

Waiting NetworkManager out. While the device state is in the connecting band (40 to 90, or 110) no override is issued, `NM is still trying - waiting it out`, and the reboot timer resets. Every rung boundary re-checks; a device reported connected without internet takes the gateway branch: `NetworkManager is connected, internet is not (round N) — router-side, no rung`.

Deletion guard. `connection down` and `connection delete` run only through one function that refuses any profile not named `awacs-(crutch|safety|join)-<epoch>-<pid>` or `awacs-joined-<hexssid>`, or stored outside `/run/NetworkManager/system-connections/`, logging `REFUSING delete: 'NAME' is not an awacs profile` or `REFUSING delete: 'NAME' lives outside /run (owner file?)`.

Names in hex. Every name comparison on nm uses lowercase hex bytes, so non-ASCII characters, colons and backslashes match exactly, and any call reaching `wpa_cli` under nm is logged as `BUG: wpa_cli reached under nm backend:` and fails.

Monitor-only mode. An unmanaged interface or a missing `nmcli` parks the daemon with a verdict that names the cure: `NetworkManager image but AWACS cannot drive it: nmcli is missing - will install it once online and restart - monitoring only` (`NetworkManager image but AWACS cannot drive it: nmcli is missing - will install it once online, then restart AWACS by hand (started with -d) - monitoring only` under `-d`) or `NetworkManager image but AWACS cannot drive it: wlan0 is unmanaged by NetworkManager - fix unmanaged-devices in NetworkManager.conf and restart AWACS - monitoring only`. It still flushes the spool, attempts the tool install and says a loss and its return once each, `internet lost on HomeNet - monitoring only, AWACS cannot recover here` and `internet back on HomeNet - monitoring only`; once `nmcli` appears, `nmcli is now installed - restarting as a full NetworkManager supervisor` and an exit so the launcher restarts it.

## Link quality and switching

Slow-link evaluation. Each healthy tick reads the interface's transmit counter over 3 seconds. A rate between 20 kbps and the floor (`MIN_UP_KBPS`, 400) for `UP_STRIKES` (3) ticks in a row, `DANCE_COOLDOWN` seconds (1200) after the last evaluation: `sustained slow upload on NAME (N kbps for 3 samples, floor 400) - evaluating known networks`, then `scan heard N networks: NAME -45, ... | known on the air: NAME, ... | stored, not heard: NAME, ...`. One probe of the current network follows, `measured N kbps on NAME - under the 400 kbps floor, a challenger must beat B kbps`; only a probe under the floor starts a comparison (above it the line reads `measured N kbps on NAME - above the 400 kbps floor, staying`), and a challenger must reach `SWITCH_GAIN_PCT` (150) percent of that probe, announced as `switching to NAME (N kbps against M kbps here)`, or the daemon returns: `no challenger beat the incumbent - staying on NAME (best was NAME at N kbps, needed B)`, `the only candidate did not deliver internet - staying on NAME at N kbps`, `none of the T candidates delivered internet - staying on NAME at N kbps` or `no other known network on the air - staying on NAME at N kbps`. A way home that fails is said (`could not return to NAME: REASON`, or `back on NAME - linked but still no internet`) and the best candidate is taken anyway, `switching to NAME (N kbps, below the B kbps bar) - the best left, HOME: REASON`; an evaluation that ends nowhere says `evaluation ended with no working network - on NAME now, the next check decides and recovery follows if the internet is gone`. Ripe strikes under the cooldown are `upload still slow on NAME (N kbps, floor 400) - evaluation on cooldown, next look in M min`, once per window. A switch that fails says so with the reason and goes back: `back on NAME`.

Live stream. While a process matching `raspistill` with `live_raw`, `preview.jpg` or `capture.jpg`, or `curl` with `upfile=@`, is running, its rate is the meter: between 5 kbps and `STREAM_MIN_KBPS` (50) for `UP_STRIKES` ticks gives `live stream starving on NAME (N kbps for 3 samples, floor 50) - evaluating known networks`, or `live stream still starving on NAME (N kbps, floor 50) - evaluation on cooldown, next look in M min` while the cooldown runs. A healthy stream is never interrupted by a probe, a switch or the preferred-network check.

Preferred-network return. Every `PREF_CHECK` seconds (600) the daemon looks for a visible stored network with a strictly higher priority (wpa `priority`, nm `connection.autoconnect-priority`). Two consecutive sightings: `higher-priority network NAME visible twice - leaving NAME to go home` (with ` (seen and lost N times before)` when it flickered first; a flicker itself is `preferred network NAME was visible at one check and gone at the next - staying on NAME until it holds for two checks in a row`, once per stay); a network that cannot be joined gives `could not reach the preferred network NAME: REASON - going back to NAME` (or `could not reach the preferred network NAME: REASON - no stored network to go back to, the next check decides`). On arrival the upload is measured, `measured N kbps on NAME`. Under the floor, `preferred network NAME too slow (N kbps, floor 400) - benched for 60 min, going back to NAME`, a veto for three cooldowns (the line ends `, ends the bench on NAME` when it ends a running bench, and reads `- benched for 60 min, staying on it` when there is no network to go back to) and `back on NAME`; a way back that fails is `could not return to NAME: REASON` then `the way back to NAME failed - on NAME now, the next check decides`; otherwise `returned to preferred network: NAME (upload N kbps)`.

Signal mode. With neither `SITE_URL` nor `PROBE_URL`, recovery joins the strongest visible stored network that delivers internet, `connected: NAME (signal mode - no upload probe target configured)`; evaluation, comparison and arrival measurement are off; the start summary reads `running with: signal mode (no probe target - networks chosen by signal), ...`.

## Scanning

Results are cached for `SCAN_TTL` seconds (30). A scan makes up to 3 attempts (6 in the first three minutes after boot); on `Device or resource busy` it waits 3 seconds once, then reads `iwlist` or the backend's own table (`wpa_cli scan_results`, `nmcli device wifi list --rescan no`). When every source returns nothing the previous picture is kept, `scan failed - using previous results (if any)`; three such scans in a row drop it, `radio heard nothing on 3 scans in a row - previous results dropped`. A scan that succeeds but names no network keeps the previous picture.

## Logging and reporting

Local log. `/var/log/awacs.log`, `yyyy-mm-ddThh:mm:ss+hh:mm [LEVEL] message`; past `LOG_CAP` (1500) plus 200 lines the file is cut back to `LOG_CAP`. `DEBUG="yes"` (the default is `no`; `AWACS_DEBUG` in the environment sets the default) adds decision-trace lines. A log that cannot be appended is reported to the site once per failure streak, `local log /var/log/awacs.log not writable - SD card read-only? the site keeps the story`, and `local log /var/log/awacs.log writable again` when appends succeed again.

Site log. With `SITE_URL` set and `LOG_TARGET` `both` or `remote`, every event is also posted to `SITE_URL/DEVICE_ID/SITE_API` as `file=log/log.txt`, formatted `yyyy-mm-ddThh:mm:ss+hh:mm [LEVEL][awacs] message` in the `SITE_TZ` zone; the text is in the language `SITE_LANG` selects (`en` by default). `remote` keeps only WARN and ERROR lines locally. The start-up line `reporting:` states the log target, the probe target and the WiFi cell setting. A site that refuses a line while the internet is up is said once per episode, `site did not take the log line (http 500) - holding lines, delivery retried about every 5 min` (`no reply in 4 s` for a timeout), and every delivery of held lines is announced after them (except a start's own flush when only its start lines were held), `delivered 9 held lines stamped 03:10 to 03:41 - they stand above this line with their own times` (`site reachable again - delivered ...` when it closes the episode).

Start report. The start line names the backend and whether this is the boot's first start, `AWACS 1.0 starting on wlan0 (device mydevice) - wpa backend, first start of this boot, up 1 min` (`restart N of this boot` after a respawn). It is followed by the settings verdict when the file was refused or missing (`settings file /etc/awacs.conf ignored: mode 644, chmod 600 it - running on built-in defaults (no site, no emergency networks)`, `settings file /etc/awacs.conf ignored: not owned by root, chown root: it - running on built-in defaults (no site, no emergency networks)`, `settings file /etc/awacs.conf not found - running on built-in defaults (no site, no emergency networks)`), the knobs that fell back (`settings file /etc/awacs.conf applied, but 2 values failed validation and use their defaults: TICK, SITE_TZ`), `wifi cell asked for (REPORT_WIFI=yes) but SITE_URL is empty - nothing to publish to`, `time zone Asia/Kuwaitt is unknown on this device (no such zone) - stamps fall back to UTC`, `device id not usable (DEVICE_ID: my camera) - reporting as cam1`, and one summary of the working settings: `running with: measured mode, floor 400/200 kbps day/night (night 22:00-06:00), switch gain 150%, one evaluation per 20 min, reboot after 30 min wedged, wifi cell on, 3 stored networks, 0 emergency, open networks yes, stamps in the device zone`. A radio found blocked says `wifi radio was soft-blocked at start - unblocked (a WLAN country not set, or a saved rfkill state)` or `wifi radio is hard-blocked - a hardware switch, AWACS cannot unblock it`. A time-sync step later on is `clock set forward 7 h 0 min by time sync - the lines above carry the old time`; a device id that changes at run time is `device id changed: cam1 -> cam2 - reporting under cam2 from now`.

Spool. Lines that cannot be sent go to `/run/awacs/spool`, at most `SPOOL_CAP` (60), the first line pinned so the outage's opening line survives. It drains in order after three healthy ticks, then every 30 ticks while lines remain. Lines the cap dropped are counted and said once the story is out: `outage story trimmed - 14 lines dropped from its middle (the spool keeps 60)`. Before a reboot the daemon orders itself the spool is copied beside the log and restored at the next start, so the outage story survives the reboot.

WiFi cell. Every six healthy ticks the daemon posts `file=tmp/wifi.tmp` with `kbps,visible,total,band,SSID`; the speed is sent only when measured on the current network, otherwise 0. `REPORT_WIFI` is `auto` (on with the site log), `yes` or `no`. A refused cell is `wifi cell not accepted by the site (http 403) - the page drops the cell when it goes stale`, once per episode, and `wifi cell accepted again` closes it. The visible count never reads below 1 while the device is associated to a stored network.

WiFi scan list. On the cell's minute, when a scan has really hit the radio since the last list (on nm, when NetworkManager's own table changed), the daemon posts `file=tmp/wifi_scan.tmp`: the scan's unix time, then up to 12 networks strongest first, each a tab-separated row of display name, signal in dBm, the upload measured on that network in this run and the time of that measurement (both empty when never measured), `known` (`1` for a stored network, `0` otherwise) and the SSID as lowercase hex, the key a site command names the network by. At most once per 60 s, never from the toolbox, no radio traffic of its own; a scan asked from the site publishes at once, in the foreground. A refused list is `wifi scan list not accepted by the site (http 500) - the list behind the WiFi box will not show`, once per episode and retried every minute, and `wifi scan list accepted again` closes it.

Tools. At start: `missing tools: iw - will try to install once online`. At the third healthy tick one background `apt-get install` per boot with the Debian package names: `installing missing tools:`, then `tools installed:` or `tool install failed - still missing:`; without apt, `no apt-get on this system - install manually:`.

## Site commands

A site with a WiFi menu can send three commands, relayed on the device into `/run/awacs/cmd` with a `SIGUSR1` that ends the daemon's sleep; the daemon reads them at the top of its main loop only, never inside a recovery or an evaluation, and answers through `file=tmp/wifi_state.tmp` as data (`taken`, `scanning`, `done N`, `trying NAME`, `measuring NAME`, `switched`, `joined`, `returned`, `failed REASON`), re-sent every healthy tick until the site takes it. The path from the tap to the first answer is about one to three seconds; the channel and the shapes are in [integration.md](integration.md).

Scan on demand. The radio is asked now, past the `SCAN_TTL` cache and the once-a-minute gate; the list is published in the foreground, then `scan from the site heard 7 networks - list published`. Offline it is `scan asked from the site while offline - the list cannot be published until the internet is back` and the answer `failed offline`; under a live stream `scan asked from the site under a live stream - a few seconds of stutter`.

Manual trial. `switch` (a stored network) and `join` (a new one, with a password typed on the site) are a trial, never a takeover: `manual trial: OfficeNet for 15 s, leaving HomeNet (1200 kbps) - it stays only at 1800 kbps or more` names the origin, its upload and the bar (the origin's upload times `SWITCH_GAIN_PCT`, the bar every challenger meets). One probe, run inside the 15 s window, decides when the window and the probe have both ended: `manual trial: OfficeNet upload 2000 kbps, the 1800 kbps bar met - staying on it`, or `manual trial: OfficeNet uploads at 700 kbps, under the 1800 kbps bar (HomeNet 1200 kbps at 150%) - going back` then `back on HomeNet`. A connect that fails is `manual trial: OfficeNet never associated - going back to HomeNet` with the connect verdict, and the answer's reason is `wrong_password`, `no_internet` or `out_of_reach`. A command that waited more than 60 s behind a recovery is `site command scan expired - it waited 2 min behind a recovery, tap again`; one that arrives during a recovery `site command switch refused - a recovery is running, tap again when the internet is back`. Every stored network is re-enabled on every exit; the ordinary evaluation and the recovery are never called from a trial. A switch may carry `hold=<seconds>` in its fifth field (the reference site's "keep me on this network" sends 1800): the trial runs the same, but a slower network that has internet is kept anyway, the answer is `switched NAME KBPS hold=<seconds>`, and for that long the preferred-network return and the evaluation do not run (`hold on OfficeNet for 30 min - no preferred-network return and no evaluation leaves it; an internet loss or a new command ends it`). A real outage, a new command or the time itself ends the hold, each said in one line (`hold on OfficeNet ended - internet lost`, `... - a new command from the site`, `... - back to its own judgement`); a restart forgets it.

Join and the password. The password travels encrypted under the device key (`/etc/awacs.key`, made once with `openssl rand`, registered with the site at start: `device key registered with the site - a password typed there can be opened here`); the daemon checks the MAC, decrypts, and adds the network at run time only (`join asked from the site: CafeNet - adding it at runtime and trying it for 15 s`): wpa `add_network` with the password over stdin and never `save_config`, nm a keyfile `awacs-join-<epoch>-<pid>` in `/run`, never `nmcli device wifi connect`. Once the link delivers internet the password is kept in `/etc/awacs.networks` (root 0600), `joined CafeNet - password kept in /etc/awacs.networks, re-added at every start`, and on nm the persistent profile `awacs-joined-<hexssid>` takes over; a wrong password removes the entry, `join: CafeNet refused - wrong password? - removing it, going back to HomeNet`. The file is re-read at start and after the rungs that restart the supplicant or NetworkManager, `re-added 1 networks joined from the site: CafeNet`; a join cut by a crash is reaped at the next start, `removed an unfinished join of CafeNet left by the previous run`. A site that holds another key (a reflash) is said once per boot, `the site holds another key for this camera (a reflash?) - open admin/wifi.php?reset_key=1 once from your phone, then join from the menu works again`. No password, key or ciphertext ever reaches a log or a site line; network names do.

## Safety and hygiene

No writes to stored configuration. No `save_config`, no `disable_network`, no `nmcli connection modify`, no `nmcli device wifi connect`; `connection down` and `delete` only through the deletion guard. Every stored network is re-enabled at start, at every recovery start, after every success, on every failed recovery's exit and at shutdown. Networks joined from the site live in the daemon's own `/etc/awacs.networks` and, on nm, in `/run` keyfiles of two classes: the trial `awacs-join-<epoch>-<pid>` (autoconnect off, swept like a crutch) and the persistent `awacs-joined-<hexssid>` (autoconnect on, kept across reaps, recreated from the file); the deletion guard accepts both names and nothing else.

Settings file. `/etc/awacs.conf` is read only when owned by root and neither readable nor writable by group or others; otherwise `awacs: IGNORING /etc/awacs.conf (group/world can access it — chmod 600 it)` or `awacs: IGNORING /etc/awacs.conf (not owned by root — chown root: it)` goes to stderr, the verdict is repeated in the log after the `reporting:` line (`settings file /etc/awacs.conf ignored: mode 644, chmod 600 it - running on built-in defaults (no site, no emergency networks)`, `settings file /etc/awacs.conf ignored: not owned by root, chown root: it - running on built-in defaults (no site, no emergency networks)`; a missing file is `settings file /etc/awacs.conf not found - running on built-in defaults (no site, no emergency networks)`) and the defaults apply. Numeric knobs must be non-zero numbers of at most seven digits, `NIGHT_START` and `NIGHT_END` must be `HH:MM`; anything else falls back to the default and is named once at start, `settings file /etc/awacs.conf applied, but 2 values failed validation and use their defaults: TICK, SITE_TZ`.

Runtime files. Everything under `RUN_DIR` (`/run/awacs`, mode 0700) is created with `umask 077`. Passwords go to the supplicant over stdin and to NetworkManager in a 0600 keyfile, never on a command line; the keys derived from the device key do pass through `openssl`'s argument list for the duration of each call, visible to root on this single-user device, which is accepted and stated. `DEVICE_ID` must match `[A-Za-z0-9_-]{1,32}`; otherwise the short hostname, then `device`, said once when a present id fails the rule: `device id not usable (DEVICE_ID: my camera) - reporting as cam1`. `flock` on `/run/awacs/lock` keeps a single instance; a second launcher's copy says `another AWACS already holds the lock (pid N) - two launchers are running it, keep one (the rc.local line or the systemd unit)` once per boot.

Stealth. `STEALTH_MODE="yes"` adds one `iptables` rule dropping ICMP echo requests on the interface and stops `avahi-daemon`: `stealth mode active (icmp hidden, avahi stopped)`, or `stealth mode partly active: iptables missing - pings still answered` when a leg failed (the parts are `iptables missing - pings still answered`, `ping rule could not be added - pings still answered` and `avahi could not be stopped`). It is not access control.

Shutdown. On SIGINT, SIGTERM, SIGQUIT or SIGHUP the daemon re-enables all networks, says `AWACS stopping on SIGTERM (systemctl stop or shutdown) - stored networks re-enabled, the network layer runs on its own until the next start` (naming the signal) and exits 0. An exit nobody ordered is caught and said: `AWACS exited unexpectedly (status 1, last command in fight: ...) - the launcher restarts it in 10 s` (`- no launcher under -d, start it again` under `-d`).

## Toolbox

The toolbox words are `status`, `networks`, `evaluate`, `scan`, `speed`, `check` and `help`; `check` and `help` run without root on the built-in defaults, not `/etc/awacs.conf`, and [README.md](../../README.md) describes the output of each word. Any other word prints `usage: awacs.sh [status|networks|evaluate|scan|speed|check|help|-d]   (no argument = daemon)`; `-d` starts the daemon in the background and `-q` is ignored.

## Day and night profile

With `NIGHT_MODE="yes"` (the default) the floor, the gain and the cooldown switch from `MIN_UP_KBPS`, `SWITCH_GAIN_PCT` and `DANCE_COOLDOWN` to `NIGHT_MIN_UP_KBPS` (200), `NIGHT_GAIN_PCT` (300) and `NIGHT_DANCE_COOLDOWN` (2400) between `NIGHT_START` (22:00) and `NIGHT_END` (06:00); the window may cross midnight. Each transition is logged once: `night profile active until 06:00 - floor 200 kbps, a challenger must reach 300% of the incumbent, evaluations 40 min apart` or `day profile active until 22:00 - floor 400 kbps, a challenger must reach 150% of the incumbent, evaluations 20 min apart`.

## Limitations

- WiFi only: one wireless interface, no Ethernet fallback; a connection needs an IPv4 address.
- Live-stream detection is hard-wired to the two process patterns above; rung L3 reloads `brcmfmac` unconditionally, which does nothing on another chip.
- The reboot cannot be switched off, only delayed; the once-per-boot package install has no off switch.
- Story lines exist in English and Arabic only (`LOG_LANG`, `SITE_LANG`); open networks are joined by default; a network where ping answers but DNS is dead reads as online.
- wpa: open networks with non-ASCII names are skipped, and stored names or `SAFETY_NET` entries containing a backslash, a double quote, a tab, a line break, an escape character or an edge space never match or are skipped.
- nm: a hidden profile must carry `hidden=yes` itself; device state 20 (unavailable) never starts the reboot timer.
